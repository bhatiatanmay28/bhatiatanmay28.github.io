Chilled Water Distribution System Design

Designed a closed-loop chilled water piping network for two buildings on the JHU Homewood Campus. Optimized pipe diameters and pump sizing to minimize total capital cost while meeting flow and velocity constraints.

Timeline Spring 2026
Course Intermediate Fluid Mechanics
Team Size 3 Members
My Role Calculations & Code

The Problem

The South Chiller Plant at Johns Hopkins needs to supply chilled water to two campus buildings through a dedicated closed-loop circuit. Each building has different flow requirements and elevation:

Building Flow Rate Device Pressure Drop Elevation
Latrobe Hall 100 gpm 200 kPa 10 m (3rd floor)
Malone Hall 50 gpm 100 kPa 5 m (2nd floor)

The goal: determine pipe diameters and pump size that minimize total capital cost (pipe material at $12/kg + pump at $1.5/W shaft power) while keeping flow velocity between 0.5 and 2.5 m/s per ASHRAE guidelines.

Image: Campus map showing chiller plant and building locations

Approach

1. Analytical Optimization

First, I derived a closed-form expression for the optimal pipe diameter. The total cost has two competing terms: pump cost scales as D⁻⁵ (smaller pipes mean more friction, more pump power), while pipe cost scales as D² (larger pipes weigh more). Setting the derivative to zero gives:

D* = (2.5 × K₁ / K₂)1/7

This showed the optimal diameter scales as (Q³L)1/7, meaning flow rate matters more than pipe length. For the Latrobe branch, this gave D* = 52.9 mm (about 2 inches).

2. Full Hydraulic Model

The analytical solution assumes constant friction factor, which introduces about 2% error. For the final design, I built a complete hydraulic model with:

  • Darcy-Weisbach equation for major losses
  • Colebrook equation for friction factor (solved by fixed-point iteration)
  • Minor losses for all fittings: elbows, tees, entrance/exit, valves
  • Parallel branch constraint (both paths must have equal pressure drop)

3. Discrete Optimization

Real pipes come in standard sizes. I wrote a MATLAB script that tested all 125 combinations of Schedule 40 pipe sizes (1.5" to 4") for the three segments: main trunk, Latrobe branch, and Malone branch. For each combination, the script:

  • Computed velocity, Reynolds number, and friction factor for each segment
  • Rejected combinations that violated the 0.5-2.5 m/s velocity constraint
  • Calculated path pressures and identified the critical (highest pressure) path
  • Sized the pump to the critical path and computed total cost

System Layout

The piping network runs from the South Chiller Plant to a junction, then splits into two parallel branches serving each building. The return lines merge and flow back to the plant.

Segment Path Length Flow
Main Trunk Chiller to Junction 340 m (round trip) 150 gpm
Latrobe Branch Junction to Latrobe 100 m (round trip) 100 gpm
Malone Branch Junction to Malone 180 m (round trip) 50 gpm

Critical Path Analysis

The Malone branch is the critical path at 588.6 kPa, despite having a lower device pressure drop. Why? The 1.5" pipe over 180 m generates 290 kPa of friction loss, which outweighs the 100 kPa device drop advantage over Latrobe.

  • Latrobe path: 181.5 + 81.9 + 200.0 = 463.4 kPa
  • Malone path: 181.5 + 307.1 + 100.0 = 588.6 kPa (critical)

The pump is sized to the critical path: 7,427 W shaft power at 75% efficiency.

Balancing Valve

Since the two branches share a common pump, the lower-resistance Latrobe path needs a balancing valve to match the Malone path pressure. The required valve coefficient is K = 60.14, which is achievable with a globe valve at about 30-40% closure.

Cost Breakdown

The design is pipe-cost dominated: 85.5% goes to piping and fittings, only 14.5% to the pump.

Sensitivity Analysis

I re-ran the full optimization at different pump cost coefficients to see how the design changes:

Pump Cost Trunk Latrobe Malone Total Cost
$1.5/W 3" 2.5" 1.5" $76,699
$2.0/W 3" 2.5" 2" $80,255
$5.0/W 3" 3" 2" $97,641
$15.0/W 4" 3" 2" $143,624

The trend is clear: as pump power becomes more expensive, the optimizer selects larger pipes to reduce friction and pump load. At $2/W, the Malone branch jumps to 2". At $15/W, even the trunk upsizes to 4".

What I Learned

  • How to set up and solve pipe network hydraulics with major and minor losses
  • The trade-off between pipe cost (scales with D²) and pump cost (scales with D⁻⁵)
  • Why closed-loop systems cancel out elevation head (what goes up must come down)
  • How to use discrete optimization when continuous solutions must map to standard sizes
  • The value of sensitivity analysis for understanding design robustness
